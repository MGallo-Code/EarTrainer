## Business Model, Pricing & The Hardware+Subscription Hybrid

The wedge is **Loop / EarLoop (C2)**: a web-first, by-ear duet engine on the MIDI keyboard users already own, expanding later into a thin iOS WebView shell plus a low-BOM USB-MIDI accessory. This section recommends concrete pricing for that product, benchmarks it against competitors, and pressure-tests the hardware+subscription hybrid against the case studies and unit-economics benchmarks in the corpus.

### Recommended pricing

| Tier | Price | What you get | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free (web)** | $0 | The duet loop, a daily by-ear drill, streaks, shareable clips — the viral surface | Web MIDI works on ~80% of devices (Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Android) [source: https://caniuse.com/midi]; zero-friction trial maximizes top-of-funnel since freemium download-to-paid is only ~2.18% [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/] |
| **Full** | **$5-8/mo, headline-billed annually (~$60-96/yr)** | Full functional curriculum, all keys/chromatics, transcription depth, teacher-grade feedback, server-side deep-analysis | Annual retains 44.1% at one year vs 17.0% monthly [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/] |
| **Hardware path (Phase 2)** | One-time device purchase → **basic access forever** | The companion USB-MIDI "duet bar" unlocks a permanently-usable basic tier without a sub; $5-8/mo still gates the full feature set | Follow The ONE / ROLI pattern, not Whoop/Snap (see below) |

The **$5-8/mo** price is deliberately at or below the music-learning floor. It undercuts Simply Piano (~$169.90/yr headline; commonly discounted to $119.99) [source: https://pianoers.com/simply-piano-review-the-honest-truth-about-learning-piano-with-an-app/] and Yousician ($19.99/mo) [source: https://tracxn.com/d/companies/yousician/__9_js14rTh79nrKlExWy_o-y8h6uzWU-JoYbthACpUYg] by roughly 60%, and sits below ear-training subscription incumbents ToneGym ($13.95/mo) and SoundGym ($24.95/mo) [source: https://www.soundgym.co/shop].

**Annual must be the headline, monthly the deliberately-worse option.** Monthly subs retain 17.0% at one year vs 44.1% for annual, ~30% of annual subs cancel in month one, and 44% of all cancellations happen within the first 90 days [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/] [source: https://churnfree.com/blog/average-churn-rate-for-subscription-services/]. That first-quarter cliff is where a hardware-driven "plug in your keyboard, do your first streak" onboarding ritual earns its keep.

### Hardware+subscription case studies: two camps

The corpus splits hybrids into a model to copy and a model to avoid.

**Camp A — hardware works without the sub (copy this):**
- **The ONE Smart Piano:** hardware purchase + always-free baseline content (sheet music, courses, games) that owners keep permanently, with optional premium and 6-12 months bundled at purchase [source: https://theonemusic.com/]. This is the closest match to the recommended model.
- **ROLI LUMI:** membership is $14.99/mo or $119.99/yr, and ROLI explicitly tells subscription-averse users to buy LUMI Keys and use it as a plain MIDI controller — hardware works without the sub [source: https://roli.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-lumi-membership]. Notably, ROLI bundles software *free* and does not put a subscription on the device itself.
- **Caveat:** ROLI entered administration in 2024 and was restructured, so its pricing is a benchmark, not a stability model.

**Camp B — hardware is hostage to the sub (avoid this):**
- **Whoop:** band is "free" but useless without an active membership (~$199-359/yr); the model dates to 2015, and the May 2025 Whoop 5.0 upgrade-policy change (fee or 12-month extension to keep "free hardware") triggered major backlash before a partial reversal [source: https://www.whoop.com/us/en/membership/]. Lesson: "free hardware forever, subsidized by the sub" is not durable as device costs rise.
- **Snap Spectacles 5:** rental-only at $99/mo developer ($49.50/mo edu), 12-month minimum, device returned if you stop paying [source: https://9to5mac.com/2024/09/17/snap-charges-99month-to-develop-for-its-new-ar-spectacles-and-snap-os/]. Maximal lock-in, but it caps the market to committed/pro users — wrong for adult self-learners who expect to own a keyboard.

**Peloton — the canonical proof the tether reduces churn:**
- Hardware runs near-breakeven (14.3% gross margin in Q3 FY2025) while subscription runs at 69% margin [source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639825/000163982525000077/q32025shareholderletter.htm].
- Connected-fitness (device-tethered) churn was just **1.2% monthly** (Q3) / 1.8% (Q4), vs. **8.1% monthly** for Peloton's app-only subs — roughly 6.8x worse churn without the hardware anchor [source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639825/000163982525000077/q32025shareholderletter.htm].
- App-only paid subs are shrinking (0.573M in Q3, -15% YoY) while device-tethered subs are stable [source: https://www.retaildive.com/news/peloton-raises-membership-pricing-holidays/761567/].

This is the load-bearing evidence for the hybrid: a physical device anchored to the app produces dramatically lower churn than app-only. Treat the duet bar as a near-breakeven CAC engine, not a profit center — Peloton-style — letting the high-margin sub carry the economics.

### Freemium conversion, churn, and CAC/LTV benchmarks

- **Conversion is the wall:** freemium download-to-paid is ~2.18% median vs 12.11% for a hard paywall; download-to-trial ~6.2% [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/]. A hardware purchase is itself a hard-paywall-equivalent — the buyer has already paid and shown intent — so hardware owners should convert to the $5-8 tier far above the 2.18% free-web baseline. That gap is the economic core of the bundle.
- **The edtech ceiling:** Duolingo, the best-in-class outlier, converts ~8% of users to paid (up from ~3% in 2020) [source: https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/inside-duolingos-6b-playbook-gamification]. A realistic new-product assumption is 2-4% on the free-web cohort at launch, climbing toward 5-8% only with strong retention loops. Treat 8% as aspirational, not expected.
- **CAC efficiency favors this category:** consumer edtech leads on payback (~3.8-month CAC payback, ~$42 CAC, ~$12 ARPU per one benchmark; treat as a single-source estimate) [source: https://www.saas.wtf/p/2024-customer-acquisition-benchmarks]. The standard target is LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 with payback under 12 months [source: https://www.paddle.com/resources/cac-ltv-ratio]. If the device sells at or above cost, it funds acquisition rather than costing it — effectively negative CAC, a structural edge over app-only rivals.
- **Web-first checkout is a real margin lever:** after the April 30, 2025 Epic v. Apple ruling, US apps could link to external web checkout at 0% Apple commission, keeping ~97% via Stripe (2.9% + $0.30) vs Apple's 15-30% [source: https://www.cravath.com/news-insights/epic-games-secures-decision-that-apple-violated-anti-steering-injunction.html]. **Caveat:** the Dec 11, 2025 Ninth Circuit ruling remanded the "reasonable fee" question to district court (Apple's SCOTUS bid reported April 2026), so the 0% window is legally unsettled [source: https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/11/apple-app-store-fees-external-payment-links/]. Design billing around web checkout, but do not assume 0% is permanent.

### Risks of tying software to hardware

- **Bricked-device resentment:** the Whoop 5.0 and Snap rental backlashes show that gating device function behind the sub generates churn-via-resentment and shrinks the market [source: https://www.whoop.com/us/en/membership/] [source: https://9to5mac.com/2024/09/17/snap-charges-99month-to-develop-for-its-new-ar-spectacles-and-snap-os/]. Hardware owners must retain real, usable value without paying — hence "basic access forever."
- **Hardware base-rate failure:** ~9% of *funded* Kickstarter hardware fails to deliver, with design and hardware/electronics issues each hitting >50% of failed campaigns [source: https://dtusciencepark.com/article/26-million-lost-why-crowdfunded-hardware-projects-fail/]. A software-first team is the exact profile that underestimates firmware/CM (cf. Triggertrap's ~12-month slip and shutdown [source: https://medium.com/triggertrap-playbook/hardware-is-hard-getting-a-kickstarter-project-shipped-59c9596bdd7f]). The viral duet clips de-risk this: they are themselves the demand signal that should gate any tooling spend, validated via crowdfunding before injection-mold MOQ commitments (500-5,000 units) [source: http://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/what-does-minimum-order-quantity-moq-mean-for-manufacturing].
- **Keep the wired/USB SKU first:** going wireless multiplies certification cost and time (~$1,500+ FCC ID per region, ~4 weeks, plus CE ~$750+) versus a wired USB-MIDI device [source: https://www.jjrlab.com/news/bluetooth-fcc-certification-cost.html]. The duet bar should be a class-compliant wired USB-MIDI accessory to keep BOM and cert cost low.
- **Thin standalone year-1 LTV:** at a low price point, year-1 realized LTV is modest — Health/Fitness median LTV/payer is $16.44, and low-priced apps median just $8.08 (vs $55.21 high-priced) [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/]. The recurring tier alone has thin year-1 economics; the model leans on the hardware margin plus the churn-reducing tether. The whole hybrid is only viable if hardware economics are favorable and the device meaningfully lengthens lifetime.
- **Software must stand alone first:** the web-first product has to carry itself before hardware ships, or a Phase-2 hardware slip stalls the entire monetization plan.

### Unit-economics sketch (stated assumptions)

Illustrative, to size the model — not a forecast. **Assumptions:** $7/mo billed annually = **$84/yr** gross; web-first Stripe checkout keeps ~97% → **~$81.50/yr net** per payer [source: https://www.cravath.com/news-insights/epic-games-secures-decision-that-apple-violated-anti-steering-injunction.html]. Annual retention 44% at one year [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/].

**Path 1 — free-web cohort (no hardware):**
- Assume 3% free-to-paid conversion (between the 2.18% freemium floor and Duolingo's 8% ceiling) [source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/] [source: https://foundercoho.substack.com/p/inside-duolingos-6b-playbook-gamification].
- Per 10,000 free signups → ~300 payers → ~$24,500 net year-1 revenue.
- At ~$42 edtech CAC, a payer costs ~$1,400 in blended ad spend per conversion at 3% (i.e., $42 / 0.03) — meaning **paid acquisition alone does not clear 3:1 LTV:CAC** at this price. Paid is a base; the organic streak/duet loop must drive blended CAC down the way Duolingo's ~80% organic model did.

**Path 2 — hardware-owner cohort:**
- Assume the duet bar sells at ~breakeven (Peloton-style 14.3% hardware GM) [source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639825/000163982525000077/q32025shareholderletter.htm], so the device sale ≈ negative/zero net CAC.
- Hardware buyers are high-intent; assume conversion well above the 2.18% baseline (Skoove's keyboard-bundle conversion is cited as far above the freemium norm) [source: https://kawai.com.au/ca-piano-bonus-offer/]. Even at a conservative 25%, each device unit yields a payer at ~$0 acquisition cost.
- Tether churn approaches Peloton's ~1.2-1.8% monthly vs ~8% app-only — so the hardware cohort's lifetime (and thus LTV) is multiples of the free-web cohort's [source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639825/000163982525000077/q32025shareholderletter.htm]. Bundling 6-12 months of full tier with each device (The ONE pattern) [source: https://theonemusic.com/] turns the sale into a pre-paid subscriber and builds the habit before the first renewal cliff.

**Conclusion:** the "buy hardware → basic access, $5-8/mo → full" model is viable and structurally de-risks churn, but only because (a) annual framing fixes the monthly-retention problem, (b) the hardware tether and bundled months lift conversion and lifetime well past free-web economics, (c) web-first checkout protects margin, and (d) the device follows the The ONE/ROLI "works without paying" pattern rather than the Whoop/Snap "brick it" model. The recurring tier alone is too thin to clear 3:1 at this price; the hardware economics and the viral organic loop are what make the math work.
