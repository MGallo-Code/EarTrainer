## Pedagogy: What Actually Builds the Skill

The evidence converges on one strategic truth: **the durable, transferable skill is relative/functional pitch (hearing scale degrees in a key), not isolated intervals and not adult perfect pitch.** Below, each evidence cluster is translated into a concrete implication for Loop's cadence-primed, MIDI-graded, shareable-by-ear loop.

### 1. Functional over interval, but for the right reason

Respected methods converge on **functional/contextual** training (recognizing each note by its function in a key) for playing by ear: Bruce Arnold's "Ear Training One Note" teaches recognition of all 12 notes relative to a key center, and Alain Benbassat's method (the free Functional Ear Trainer) teaches notes by their function rather than by interval [source: https://muse-eek.com/ear-training-one-note-for-musicians-book-with-audio/].

The honest framing matters. The popular vendor claim that "interval training fails because pitch is context-dependent" is only *modestly* supported by the lab data, not the slam-dunk vendors imply. Graves & Oxenham (2017, Frontiers) found a familiar tonal context improves interval discrimination by a *small* margin: a d' improvement of 0.47 (Cohen's d = 1.02) for melodic context and 0.28 (Cohen's d = 0.59) for harmonic context, and the authors explicitly call the benefit "relatively small," suggesting it primes expectation more than perception [source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01753/full]. The stronger case is about *transfer to real music*: Dowling's work shows inexperienced listeners use context-invariant interval representations, while moderately experienced listeners shift to scale-step (functional) representations [source: http://mp.ucpress.edu/content/3/3/281]. Functional training accelerates the representation experts naturally adopt.

> **Product implication:** Make functional, scale-degree grading the spine of every duet card (this is C3's contribution to the wedge). Lead with it, position interval drills as a secondary mode. But market on **transfer** ("hear notes the way working musicians do"), not on overstated "intervals are useless" perceptual claims, which a skeptic can refute.

### 2. Always prime the key first (cadence-before-item)

Establishing tonal context before each test item measurably improves perception across multiple lab studies; the same Graves & Oxenham result confirms context beats isolation (small-to-moderate effect) [source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5640898/]. This is direct justification for priming a key with a cadence rather than playing notes cold.

> **Product implication:** Every By-Ear Duet Card opens with a I-IV-V cadence to establish the tonic before the user finds the part. This is the load-bearing UX pattern that makes the social object a *learning* object, not a guessing game. (Note: the corpus's "melodic d~1.02" figure is the headline Cohen's d from this study; the absolute perceptual benefit is small, so the cadence is justified but should not be oversold as transformative.)

### 3. The teachable by-ear skill stack is sequential

The practitioner consensus stack is well-defined: **find the tonic → hear scale degrees in the key → chord function (V-I, then V7-I, then IV-V-I) → melodic and rhythm dictation → transcription as the capstone** [source: https://www.key-notes.com/blog/play-piano-by-ear]. Dictation is empirically a teachable, multi-component skill: Nichols & Springer (2025) found tonal working memory (β=0.406), years of piano lessons (β=0.410), and aural-skills coursework (β=0.353) jointly predict melodic dictation, but the full model explains only R²=0.365 of variance (N=34) [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12341474/]. ~64% is unexplained, meaning a large trainable component exists beyond raw aptitude, and piano background helps even non-pianists.

> **Product implication:** Sequence the duet content along this stack rather than scattering random drills. Onboard with tonic-finding and tonic-triad cards; gate progression toward V-I → IV-V-I before unlocking full melodic transcription. Frame transcription (play-back-by-ear on the MIDI keyboard) as the flagship capstone, the skill audio-only apps cannot grade and the one that produces shareable duet content.

### 4. Rhythm is a distinct, separately-trained skill, with a caveat

Rhythm/meter internalization is foundational, but the evidence is narrower than blanket "attend to rhythm first" advice. Paney's research found that directing students to attend to rhythm first improved *rhythm* accuracy specifically in *polyphonic* dictation, while pitch accuracy was unaffected, and broader "direct your attention" instructions actually *lowered* overall scores [source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735614547409].

> **Product implication:** Train rhythm as its own card type, and let MIDI timing capture grade it objectively (free, exact). But do not over-coach attention mid-drill, the evidence says general attention-direction backfires. Keep rhythm grading present but lightweight; don't bolt verbose "focus on X" prompts onto every item.

### 5. Solfege: a real product fork with conflicting evidence

Counterintuitively, fixed-do beat movable-do on sight-singing *pitch accuracy* with very large effect sizes across all three complexity levels (Hung 2012, N=85: 45 fixed-do vs 40 movable-do) [source: https://repository.usfca.edu/diss/38/]. But this measures sight-singing accuracy, a *different construct* from ear-to-instrument transfer. Movable-do (and scale-numbers) is the system that actually maps to scale-degree/functional hearing, the skill that underlies playing by ear.

> **Product implication:** Default to **movable-do / scale-numbers** (it teaches the functional hearing the wedge depends on, and matches how the beachhead pop/jazz self-learners already think), but the fixed-do result is a warning: don't force one label system on classically-trained users, who often use fixed-do. Offer a label toggle. Do not cite Hung as evidence movable-do is "better"; it isn't, for what it measured.

### 6. Adult perfect pitch: a labeled novelty, never a promise

Adults can acquire *partial* AP, but it is rare and hard-won. Van Hedger et al. (2019, PLOS ONE): 2 of 6 self-selected adults reached genuine-AP levels after ~32 hours over 8 weeks, retained at 4-month follow-up, and the authors questioned whether it was "true" AP [source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0223047]. Wong et al. (2025, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review): adults learned ~7 of 12 pitches at 90% accuracy within ~2s after 25 hours, with +128% accuracy and -43% error magnitude, and partial transfer to untrained instruments [source: https://featuredcontent.psychonomic.org/from-pitchy-to-pitch-perfect-training-absolute-pitch-in-adults/]. AP is genuinely rare (~4% of music students) and critical-period bound: 40% of musicians who began training by age 4 report AP versus 3% who started at age 9+ (Deutsch et al. 2006, not Wikipedia, which mis-attributes this) [source: https://absolutepitch.org/studies/deutsch-2006.html].

> **Product implication:** Sell *relative* pitch. Never promise adults perfect pitch, it invites refunds and credibility damage. At most, ship an optional, clearly-labeled "pitch-class familiarity" mini-mode for power users, citing the partial gains honestly. This stays consistent with the wedge: the moat is graded by-ear/improv data, not an AP gimmick.

### 7. Spaced repetition: schedule the review, don't just drill randomly

The spacing effect transfers to musical material: spaced practice beat massed practice for song retention (Katz, Ando & Wiseheart 2021, Cognitive Research), with a 2-day gap already sufficient [source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34894323/]. Most ear-trainers drill randomly.

> **Product implication:** Build an SRS scheduler over the user's *weak* scale degrees, shaky keys, and unstable chord functions, resurfacing them on expanding intervals. This is a defensible, evidence-grounded edge over the random-drill incumbents (Perfect Ear, Complete Ear Trainer) and pairs naturally with the longitudinal per-user dataset that is the wedge's moat.

### 8. Deliberate practice quality > hours; gamification is retention, not transfer

Deliberate practice explains only ~21% of variance in music performance (Macnamara et al. 2014 meta-analysis: 21% music, 26% games, 18% sports) [source: https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Macnamara-et-al.-2014.pdf]. Quantity alone is oversold; practice *design* (targeted weak-spot detection, immediate feedback, adaptive difficulty) carries more weight. Gamification boosts engagement in music education, but the rigorous studies are mostly in children (ages 6-12), and the specific paired control figures circulated online conflate two separate studies, so treat them as directional, not proven for adult by-ear transfer [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12865349/].

> **Product implication (two-part):**
> - **Coach on quality:** Surface a "what to practice next" engine driven by MIDI-exact weak-spot detection, not just a streak counter. Deliberate-practice quality is the lever the corpus says matters most.
> - **Gamify for retention only:** Use streaks, leagues, and the duet/share loop as a *motivation wrapper* (consistent with EarLoop's virality spine), but keep the layer cosmetic over a pedagogically serious core. Tune for adult self-learners (the under-studied segment), not kid-oriented designs. Do not market gamification as a learning *accelerator*, the evidence doesn't support that claim for adults.

### 9. The mastery-gate progression model

The Benbassat / Functional Ear Trainer pattern, recommending ~10 min/day and progressing from a partial scale to a full octave to multiple octaves to new scales, is a proven, copyable spine [source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/functional-ear-trainer/id1088761926]. Note: the often-quoted exact mechanics ("Level 1 = 4 notes, 90% accuracy gate") are an unverified design detail per the corpus, so adopt mastery-gating as the *principle* without claiming those precise thresholds as established fact.

> **Product implication:** Gate duet-card difficulty on demonstrated mastery (advance only when accuracy holds), expand from a subset of degrees → full scale → chromatics → new keys, and layer the SRS scheduler on top. This makes the by-ear engine "honest" (mastery-gated, functionally graded), which is exactly what lets the shareable duet card be a learning object rather than a karaoke gimmick.
