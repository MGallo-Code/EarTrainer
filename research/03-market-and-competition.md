## Market & Competitive Landscape

### Market size and growth

- **Online music education is a mid-single-digit-billions market growing in the low-to-mid teens.** Mordor Intelligence sizes it at ~$3.9B in 2025, rising to ~$9.36B by 2031 at a 15.23% CAGR; piano is the largest instrument segment (~38.85% of 2025 revenue) and app-based platforms are already ~50.75% of revenue, with North America at ~35.05% [source: https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/online-music-education-market]. Treat these as one vendor's model, not consensus; competing firms give materially different numbers.
- **Anchor on credible sources, not SEO mills.** Music-learning-app TAM estimates cluster around ~$1.5B-$2.1B for 2024 with ~13-14% CAGRs; the $20B figures that float around conflate broader "online music education" or "music products" categories [source: https://growthmarketreports.com/report/music-learning-apps-market]. MIDI-controller market sizing is unreliable, with 2024 estimates ranging ~$540M to ~$1.82B and CAGRs ~4.8%-10.2% [source: https://www.strategicmarketresearch.com/market-report/midi-controller-market].
- **Willingness to pay for piano-learning subscriptions is proven at nine figures.** Simply (formerly JoyTunes, maker of Simply Piano) raised ~$97M total and hit a $1B valuation in its 2021 Series D ($50M led by GV, with Qualcomm and Hearst); Latka estimates ~$200M revenue for 2024 (an estimate, not audited) [source: https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-joytunes-raising-50m-at-1b-valuation-1001375306]. Sensor Tower's recent-month US estimates show Simply Piano at ~400K iOS downloads / ~$5M revenue and ~800K Android installs / ~$1M revenue, illustrating the iOS-pays / Android-installs split [source: https://app.sensortower.com/overview/1019442026?country=US].
- **The hardware tailwind is real but slower.** NAMM sizes the global music products industry at ~$17B (the $20B figure is unsourced/overstated), with US digital/electronic instruments up 36.5% cumulatively over the last decade and continued 2024 growth [source: https://www.namm.org/blog/industry-insights-key-takeaways-2025-global-report]. Digital pianos are a ~$1.07-1.23B (2023-2024) market growing ~5.5-5.8% CAGR toward ~$1.8-2.2B by 2033 [source: https://www.sphericalinsights.com/reports/digital-piano-market]. Digital dominates entry-level buyers, though the often-cited "65% of beginners choose digital" stat is unsupported [source: https://www.worldpianonews.com/musings/digital-piano-supply-shortage-this-christmas/].
- **Reach is essentially universal in the target demo, validating web-first.** 91% of US adults own a smartphone (97% ages 18-29, 96% ages 30-49; 97% of $100K+ households) [source: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/].

**Implication for the Loop wedge:** the dollars sit in piano-learning subscriptions (Simply's scale proves it), not in dedicated ear-training, which anchors low. Loop's by-ear engine is the differentiator, but the path to the larger "play songs I love" demand runs through the piano segment Simply dominates.

### Where incumbents are weak

- **Nobody does play-by-ear.** The 8 surveyed piano apps (Simply, Yousician, Flowkey, Skoove, Synthesia, Playground, Piano Marvel, Melodics) teach "follow the falling notes / sheet music," not playing by ear; Simply explicitly omits playing by ear, improvisation, and advanced theory even at $169.90/yr [source: https://beardedskeptic.com/review/simply-piano-review/]. The dedicated ear-trainers (Perfect Ear, Functional Ear Trainer, Tenuto) are tap-to-identify recognition tools, never play-back-graded. EarMaster is the one exception that grades played/sung input, but it is "clunky and dated" with overly broad scope [source: https://sonofield.com/blog/best-ear-training-apps-2026].
- **Nobody does record-and-layer.** No major learning app offers multitrack record-plus-layer as a core feature; users get pushed to DAWs (this is one survey's app-by-app read, directionally credible but not independently reproduced) [source: https://www.toptenreviews.com/piano-apps-to-advance-your-learning-practice-tracking-notation-and-backing-track-software]. This is the literal phrase in the product vision and a near-total category gap.
- **Mic detection is the structural weakness MIDI beats.** Simply Piano's own help center tells users to switch to MIDI to fix chord/polyphonic recognition, and Playground Sessions deliberately abandoned pitch detection because it fails on polyphony [source: https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/playground-sessions-review]. Mic-first incumbents (Simply, Yousician, Skoove) therefore could not produce clean stitched clips even if they bolted on a duet loop; MIDI quantizes a beginner's sloppy timing into a shareable result. (Caveat: in a quiet room Simply's mic detection is reviewed as near-perfect including chords, so the MIDI advantage is conditional, not absolute [source: https://beardedskeptic.com/review/simply-piano-review/].)
- **"Feedback theater" and dated UX are the loudest, most repeated complaints.** Flowkey can mark a lesson "complete" after all-wrong notes [source: https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/flowkey-review]; the recurring complaint set across the field is dated/clunky UIs (EarMaster, ToneGym), basic/plain UI (Functional Ear Trainer), and iOS crashes (Perfect Ear) [source: https://sonofield.com/blog/best-ear-training-apps-2026]. A polished web-first product can win on craft alone.
- **No organic/UGC growth loop, and grading creative playing is unsolved.** Music-app growth is paid-acquisition-led (Flowkey doubled ROAS via Apple Search Ads, +80% downloads; Yousician via YouTube TrueView) [source: https://splitmetrics.com/cases/flowkey-apple-search-ads-optimization/]; only Duolingo (general learning, ~80% organic) and Smule (singing UGC, ~20M videos/day) have real viral loops, neither in piano. Separately, AI grading of a user's own improvisation/by-ear playing is an open, developing problem [source: https://www.soundverse.ai/blog/article/ai-practice-tools-for-musicians] — the basis for Loop's data moat.
- **One incumbent is probing "beyond the notes" but left the audio/by-ear lane open.** ROLI's AI Music Coach launched February 2026 using an infrared camera tracking 27 hand joints at 90fps to judge fingering/posture, not MIDI and not by-ear [source: https://newatlas.com/music/roli-ai-music-coach-piano/]. It validates that incumbents are extending past note-accuracy while leaving the by-ear/harmonic dimension uncontested.
- **The contested adjacencies are the price floor and the bundle channel.** Ear-training is anchored to free/one-time pricing (Tenuto $4.99 once [source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tenuto/id459313476]; EarMaster ~$3.30-5.50/mo with a free tier), so a $5-8/mo ask must make the by-ear value obvious. The keyboard-bundle channel (Kawai stacks Skoove + Tomplay + Piano Marvel codes; 6+ apps run bundles) is a proven near-zero-CAC funnel but already crowded — a web-first "scan QR, play instantly, no install" pitch is the differentiated angle [source: https://kawai.com.au/ca-piano-bonus-offer/].

**Net white space:** 0 of 8 piano apps teach by-ear, 0 offer record/layer, and the dedicated ear-trainers never grade what you actually play back. The intersection of functional by-ear grading + MIDI record/layer + a shareable duet loop ships nowhere — exactly the Loop wedge.
